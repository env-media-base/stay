INN 002 ISUMI · CHIBA 2026.03

Kayabuki-an, a 120-Year-Old Thatched Kominka

A thatched house with an irori (sunken hearth), rented whole, standing quietly amid the rice fields of Isumi.

Twenty minutes along the paddy paths from a tiny unstaffed station on the Isumi Railway, the view suddenly opens up — and a thatched roof appears. Built 120 years ago, this was once the home of the village headman. Three years ago, the current owner, a grandchild of the family, brought the near-derelict house back to life as a whole-house rental inn.

Rice Cooked in a Clay Pot Over the Irori

In the evening, a fire is lit in the irori (sunken hearth). The firewood crackles. A clay pot is hung over the flames, and local rice goes on to cook. The aroma drifts with the steam through the whole house.

Meals are a set of home-style dishes prepared ahead by local mothers: simmered vegetables, grilled fish, pickles. Nothing extravagant — and that is exactly the point.

The Engawa and the Breeze off the Rice Fields

In the morning, you sit on the engawa (wooden veranda) with a cup of tea. Rice paddies stretch out before you. When the wind blows, the ears of rice sway all at once. Frogs calling, a rooster crowing somewhere in the distance.

Before this house was left with no one living in it, I wanted someone to see it. — Sato-san, the owner

You stop checking the clock. Somehow, you stop checking your phone too. Few places let you spend time doing nothing quite this luxuriously.

One Group a Day, the Whole House to Yourselves

Kayabuki-an hosts just one group per night, with exclusive use of the entire house. For that day, the whole place belongs to you.

It is by no means cheap. Yet split among a family or a group of four friends, it works out to the ¥10,000-range per person. And above all, there is time here you simply cannot spend anywhere else.
