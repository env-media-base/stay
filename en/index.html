ABOUT US

Inns you don't just stay at —
inns you meet.

Kakureyado uncovers inns across Chiba that never appear on the major booking sites,
and quietly shares them — owners, stories and all.

01

Not on any
booking site

We only feature inns you can't reach through the big booking platforms — small, local places tucked away across Chiba that the algorithms never surface.

02

Experiences you'll find
nowhere else

A seaside minshuku kept going by its owner alone. A 100-year-old kominka (old folk house). An inn where you split your own firewood at dawn. Not travel to consume — time you'll remember.

03

Before they
disappear

Many of these inns face closure — no successor, too few guests. Before they're gone, we want you to see the view from their windows and feel the warmth of the hands that keep them.

Tateyama Seaside
INN  001 TATEYAMA

A seaside inn in Tateyama
Minshuku Nagi

Local fish landed that very morning, and sunrise over the Pacific. A tiny four-room inn run single-handedly by its okami (the innkeeper).

NO. 02
Isumi Kominka
INN  002 ISUMI

A 120-year-old thatched farmhouse
Kayabuki-an

Rice cooked over the irori (sunken hearth), and the breeze on the veranda. One slow night amid the rice fields, with your watch set aside.

NO. 03
Otaki Mountains
INN  003 YOUROU

A mountain lodge in Yoro Valley
Komorebi

Split your own firewood in the morning; fall asleep to the sound of the gorge under a sky full of stars. Three days of breathing in time with the forest, electricity kept to a minimum.

INN  004 SAWARA

A townhouse inn in "Little Edo" Sawara
Sawara no Yado

Edo-era streetscapes along the Ono River. An evening in a restored Meiji-period merchant house — a private inn for just two parties at a time.
